If you are tracking heart rate variability to guide a recovery, the device you measure with matters more than almost any other choice you make about your routine. Not because a better sensor gives you a *nicer* number, but because HRV is built entirely out of the timing between heartbeats, and timing is the one thing optical sensors are worst at.

**A chest strap fixes that, and both the Polar H10 and the cheaper COOSPO H808S do the job well.** Here is why a strap matters, how these two compare, which one fits you, and how to use either one with Autonomic.

## Why does a chest strap beat a watch for HRV?

Your watch, your ring and your phone's camera all measure your pulse the same way: they shine light into your skin and watch the reflection change as blood moves underneath. That technique, photoplethysmography, is very good at answering "what is your heart rate right now." It is much shakier at answering "exactly how many milliseconds passed between that beat and the one before it" ([Schäfer and Vagedes, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcard.2012.03.119)).

HRV is nothing but that second question, asked hundreds of times in a row.

A chest strap does not look at blood at all. Two electrode strips sit against your ribcage and pick up the electrical impulse that fires your heart, the same signal a clinical ECG records. The moment of the beat is unambiguous, so the interval between beats is measured rather than estimated.

<div class="callout callout-note">
<strong>Why the error compounds.</strong> RMSSD, the core HRV metric, is calculated from the differences between consecutive beat intervals (<a href="https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2017.00258" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Shaffer and Ginsberg, 2017</a>). A timing wobble of 10 or 15 milliseconds is invisible in your heart rate, but it is the same size as the real signal you are trying to see. Small timing noise becomes large HRV noise.
</div>

This is why validation research keeps reaching for the same reference device, and why the gap between strap and wrist shows up so clearly in the numbers. We go through that evidence in [how accurate HRV wearables really are](/insights/research/how-accurate-are-hrv-wearables/) and compare device classes in [the best way to measure HRV at home](/insights/hrv/how-to-measure-hrv-at-home-devices/). When researchers checked recent Apple Watches against a Polar H10, single HRV readings were off by about 20 ms on average, far outside the 10 ms margin they had set as acceptable ([O'Grady et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/s24196220)).

A strap is especially worth it if you do [orthostatic stand tests](/insights/pots/the-orthostatic-stand-test-at-home/), where beat timing during a position change is exactly what you are trying to capture, or [resonant breathing work](/insights/hrv/resonant-breathing-hrv-biofeedback/), where you want to see coherence build in real time.

## Is the Polar H10 worth the price?

The <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PM54P4N?tag=autonomic03-20" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" target="_blank" data-cta="affiliate_amazon_h10_intro">Polar H10</a> is the default answer, and it has earned that position. When a study needs a consumer device to stand in for clinical equipment, this is usually it. In one study of ten people doing activities from rest to high intensity, the H10's beat intervals correlated with a Holter ECG at r = 0.997, with 99.6% signal quality ([Gilgen-Ammann et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-019-04142-5)). A later study found strong agreement and small bias for standard HRV measures at rest and during exercise ([Schaffarczyk et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/s22176536)).

What you are paying for, beyond the sensor:

- **Onboard memory.** It can record one session of up to 30 hours on its own, without a phone nearby ([Polar](https://www.polar.com/us-en/products/accessories/h10_heart_rate_sensor)).
- **Bluetooth and ANT+.** It can feed the Autonomic app and a bike computer or gym watch.
- **The band.** This is the underrated part. The Polar Pro Strap is wider and softer, with a silicone grip pattern that keeps it from sliding. Over a long session, or overnight, you notice. It also comes in a wider range of sizes, from small to very large chests.
- **Longevity.** Build quality is the reason people keep recommending H10s that are five years old. Polar rates the battery at up to 400 hours on a replaceable CR2025.

Polar's US store listed it at $89.21 when we checked on November 13, 2026; prices move with sales, so check before you buy. The band is the part that wears, and replacements are cheap when it eventually stretches.

## Is the COOSPO H808S as accurate as the Polar H10?

We did not expect much from the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07R8741CN?tag=autonomic03-20" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" target="_blank" data-cta="affiliate_amazon_coospo_intro">COOSPO H808S</a>. It costs a fraction of the Polar, and it is a no-name brand to most people. We expected to write a paragraph explaining why the extra money was obviously worth it.

That is not what happened. Worn alongside the Polar H10, the H808S produced morning readings we could not meaningfully separate. Same beat-to-beat data over Bluetooth, same stability once the electrodes were wet, no dropouts sitting still for a five-minute reading.

Be clear about what that rests on: our own use. The Polar has published validation studies behind it; we could not find one for the COOSPO. For the specific job of a resting reading each morning, it held up for us, but it is weaker evidence than the Polar's.

Two things set it apart in daily use:

- **It beeps.** COOSPO's manual says it gives two beeps once it detects your heartbeat and one beep when you take it off ([COOSPO H808S manual](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0636/7749/7600/files/COOSPO-H808S-User-Manual.pdf)). If you have ever finished a reading and realized the strap had silently lost contact, you will appreciate it. If you are measuring at 6am next to a sleeping partner, you will not.
- **The band is more basic.** It is thinner and less grippy than the Polar Pro Strap. Sitting or lying still, this is a non-issue. Moving around, it is more likely to need a readjustment.

COOSPO rates it at 300 hours on a replaceable CR2032, with Bluetooth and ANT+ and an IP67 rating. COOSPO's own store listed it at $40.59 on sale when we checked on November 13, 2026, and it is often cheaper at other retailers.

## Which strap should you buy?

**Pick the Polar H10 if:**

- you want the device the research uses, the benchmark with nothing to justify;
- you will wear it for workouts, long sessions or overnight, where comfort compounds;
- you want to record without your phone nearby;
- you measure next to someone sleeping, since it makes no sound;
- your chest is small or very large, since Polar makes more band sizes.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07PM54P4N?tag=autonomic03-20" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" target="_blank" data-cta="affiliate_amazon_h10_pick">See the Polar H10 on Amazon</a>

**Pick the COOSPO H808S if:**

- you mostly take a two to five minute reading in bed or a chair each morning;
- the price difference decides whether you own a strap at all;
- you like an audible signal that the strap has good contact.

<a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07R8741CN?tag=autonomic03-20" rel="sponsored nofollow noopener" target="_blank" data-cta="affiliate_amazon_coospo_pick">See the COOSPO H808S on Amazon</a>

A COOSPO on your chest every morning beats a Polar you did not buy, by an enormous margin, and it beats a wrist reading by nearly as much. The genuinely wrong answer is spending months agonizing over the choice while measuring with a watch. For stand tests, either one works; wet electrodes matter more than the brand.

<div class="callout callout-warn">
<strong>Get the electrodes wet.</strong> This is the whole game. A dry strap gives noisy, jumpy data on any brand, and it is the most common reason people conclude their strap is broken. Run both electrode strips under a tap before you put it on, position it directly under the chest muscle, and snug it so it does not slide.
</div>

## Does it work with the Autonomic app?

**Yes. Autonomic reads any Bluetooth strap that sends the standard Heart Rate Service with beat-to-beat (RR interval) data, and both the Polar H10 and the COOSPO H808S do.** There is nothing to install and no account to link.

1. Wet the electrodes and put the strap on.
2. Open a Live HRV reading in Autonomic.
3. Tap the source row and pick your strap from the nearby-devices list.

Autonomic remembers the strap after that, so tomorrow's reading starts with one tap. Each morning reading becomes a score out of 100 against your own baseline, which is what turns a clean number into a decision about your day. ## How do you get a clean reading from either strap?

The strap removes the sensor as a source of error, which leaves your routine as the remaining one. HRV moves with posture, breathing, digestion, caffeine and time of day, and those swings are much bigger than any difference between these two devices.

So fix the routine: same time each morning, same posture, before coffee and before food. ## The bottom line

A chest strap is the highest-leverage purchase in home HRV tracking, because it removes the largest source of error in the metric. The Polar H10 is the validated benchmark and worth its price if comfort, memory or certainty matter to you. The COOSPO H808S costs well under half as much and, in our hands, gave up almost nothing for daily resting readings, though no published study backs that up yet. Both work with Autonomic. Get either, wet the electrodes, and measure the same way every morning.
