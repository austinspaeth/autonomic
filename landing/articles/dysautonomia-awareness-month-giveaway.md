## Why October matters

Dysautonomia Awareness Month was started by [Dysautonomia International](https://www.dysautonomiainternational.org/) in 2012, and its symbol is a turquoise ribbon. "Dysautonomia" is an umbrella term for conditions where the autonomic nervous system, the part that runs your body without asking, stops doing its job smoothly. [POTS](/insights/pots/is-it-pots-or-anxiety/) is the one many people know. It also covers neurocardiogenic syncope, autonomic neuropathies, multiple system atrophy, and the autonomic problems that have followed so many people out of [long COVID](/insights/postviral/is-it-long-covid-or-pots/).

The numbers are why a month exists at all:

  <div class="gvx-stat"><span class="gvx-stat-n" data-to="70" data-suf="M+">70M+</span><span class="gvx-stat-d">people worldwide live with some form of dysautonomia, by Dysautonomia International's estimate</span></div>
  <div class="gvx-stat"><span class="gvx-stat-n">1–3M</span><span class="gvx-stat-d">Americans are estimated to have POTS alone, before the post-COVID wave is counted</span></div>
  <div class="gvx-stat"><span class="gvx-stat-n" data-to="2" data-suf=" yrs">2 yrs</span><span class="gvx-stat-d">median wait from first seeing a doctor to a POTS diagnosis, in a survey of 4,835 patients</span></div>
  <div class="gvx-stat"><span class="gvx-stat-n" data-to="15" data-suf="%">15%</span><span class="gvx-stat-d">of those patients waited more than ten years for their answer</span></div>

That last pair comes from the largest patient survey of POTS to date ([Shaw et al., 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joim.12895)). The good news in the same paper is that the wait has been shrinking. Awareness is a big part of why, and it is why this month is worth showing up for.

## Why readings, and not just retweets

Most giveaways ask you to follow, like and share. We ask for readings because of what they add up to. One HRV reading is a snapshot. **A month of mornings is a pattern:** your own [baseline](/insights/hrv/hrv-baseline-how-to-build-and-use-it/), how it dips after a bad night or a hard day, and what seems to bring it back up. Heart rate variability is one of the few windows we have into how your autonomic nervous system is doing, and it means the most when you compare it to yourself.

That matters most in the doctor's office. "I feel worse after I stand for a while" is easy to wave off. A month of readings is much harder to dismiss, and Autonomic can turn yours into a [summary to take to your appointment](/insights/recovery/turn-your-data-into-a-doctor-conversation/).

### Five habits that make ten mornings count

1. **Same time, same position.** Right after you wake, sitting or lying the same way each day. Posture changes HRV more than most people expect ([here is why](/insights/hrv/hrv-standing-vs-lying-down-posture/)).
2. **Before the coffee.** Caffeine, a shower and your phone's notifications all move the number.
3. **Breathe normally.** A morning baseline is an unpaced reading. Save the slow breathing for a training session.
4. **Light pressure on the camera.** If you use your phone, rest your fingertip gently. Pressing hard squeezes out the very pulse the camera is trying to see.
5. **Do not chase a good number.** A low morning is information, not a failure. Some of the most useful readings in a month are the bad ones.
